Honam (호남) Semiconductor Cluster · Gwangju military airport (광주 군공항)

The land fits. The timetable is the risk.

Samsung Electronics and SK hynix plan four memory fabs and about ₩800 trillion of investment on Gwangju's flat, state-owned airbase[S1, S3]. Production is targeted for June 2030, but only if 6.3 GW of power, 650 000 t/d of water and the airbase's departure arrive in sequence[S7, S11, S17].

Evidence

Close to its inputs, far from its industry, and the numbers disagree

Water, power and rail sit within about 50 km. The capital-region supply base is over 180 km away, and official figures for the grid and water differ by source.

F1 · Water supply

Mixed

Three dams plus treated wastewater are to supply 650 000 t/d by 2030; most of the originally planned dam water comes from the drought-exposed Seomjin basin[S8, S9].

Summary

The government states the four fabs need 650 000 t of industrial water per day at full build-out, and the climate-energy-environment minister told the October 2026 National Assembly audit this is deliverable[S11]. The August 2026 revision of the supply plan uses three dams and treated wastewater: stage 1 (Dongbok Dam (동복댐) and Juam Dam (주암댐), 350 000 t/d) and stage 2 (Naju Dam (나주댐) plus 200 000 t/d of reclaimed water from the Gwangju No. 1 sewage treatment plant, about 3 km from the site), to be completed by 2030[S8]. Reclaimed water is earmarked for cooling; ultrapure water (UPW) for wafer cleaning is to come from dam and river water, because sewage-derived feed is hard to bring to the ≤1 ppb total-organic-carbon level UPW requires[S8]. Contrary to the starting lead that water would come from the Yeongsan River (영산강) system, a National Assembly Research Service (NARS) review found that 84.6 % of the originally planned dam water (550 000 t/d) would come from the Seomjin River (섬진강) basin, which recorded severe drought storage levels in 2022 and 2026[S9]. The city of Gwangju separately cites an interim 150 000 t/d by end-2028 and a long-run 1.06 Mt/d, which differs from the national figure[S7].

Assessment

▲ Advantage
Three existing dams plus reclaimed water close to the site; preliminary feasibility study waived[S7, S8].
▼ Constraint
No dedicated industrial-water line to Gwangju today; pipelines and Dongbok raising still to be built[S10, S7].
? Uncertainty
Drought yield of Seomjin-basin dams; UPW feedstock quality[S9, S8].

Disputes and open questions

  • Drought resilience: a NARS fact-check cited by Rep. Koo Ja-keun (People Power Party, PPP) says the volumes' calculation basis was not disclosed and that dependence on one basin is a drought risk[S9].
  • Minister Kim Sung-hwan and Rep. Shin Jeong-hoon (Democratic Party, DP) argue Yeongsan and Seomjin water has mostly gone to agriculture and that supply is adequate[S11].
  • Whether reclaimed wastewater can feed UPW production at scale is unresolved; the ministry currently limits it to cooling[S8].
  • National (650 000 t/d by 2030) and city (1.06 Mt/d long-run) targets differ; the relationship between them is not explained in the sources[S7, S8].

F2 · Electric power

Mixed

Generation is close by, but a new 345 kV link must be built faster than recent 345 kV projects[S11, S12].

Summary

The fabs need 6.3 GW at full build-out; one projection rises from 1.57 GW in 2031 to 6.28 GW in 2034[S11, S13]. Generation is nearby: the Hanbit nuclear plant (한빛원전) in Yeonggwang (영광) has 5 875 MW installed, and the minister says 3–5 GW of Honam power is currently exported to the capital region or curtailed[S14, S11]. The binding constraint is transmission. In the October 2026 audit, the government said the stage-1 supply line target had moved from end-2029 to 2028, using a new branch of about 32 km off the Hanbit–Sin-Gwangju (신광주) 345 kV line[S11]. KEPCO describes stage 1 as 20–25 km of 345 kV line costing ₩1.4 trillion, over 80 % paid by Samsung and SK hynix, while another report gives about 43 km for the full build[S12, S13]. The opposition questions renewables' peak contribution (solar 14.3 %, wind 2.4 % under the 11th Basic Plan) and the schedule, given KEPCO's own ~9-year standard construction period for 345 kV works[S11, S12].

Assessment

▲ Advantage
Large nuclear and renewable output near the site, now partly exported or curtailed[S11, S14].
▼ Constraint
New 345 kV line and substation needed; 2028 target is tight against historical build times[S11, S12].
? Uncertainty
Peak-hour firm capacity; Hanbit unit retirements; final route and length[S10, S11].

Disputes and open questions

  • Peak-hour reliability: Rep. Lee Jong-bae (PPP) argued that solar and wind together deliver under 1 GW at peak; Minister Kim Sung-hwan and Rep. Shin Jeong-hoon (DP) cite surplus Honam generation now exported north[S11].
  • Schedule: Rep. Kim Do-eup (PPP) cited KEPCO's ~9-year standard period; KEPCO CEO Kim Dong-cheol said completion in 2–3 years is possible[S12].
  • Redundancy: Rep. Jeong Jin-wook (DP) called for a duplicated supply; KEPCO said its grid has few single-path links[S12]. Experts call for triple redundancy[S13].
  • Reported line lengths (20–25 km, ≈32 km, ≈43 km) and stage-1 capacities (3.1 GW vs ≈4 GW) differ between sources; the route and design were described as at a 'basic review stage'[S11, S12, S13, S7].
  • Hanbit units 1–2 reached design life (unit 1 stopped; unit 2 to stop Sept 2026) and units 3–6 retire from 2034 unless extended[S10].

F3 · Land and site

Advantage

A flat, state-owned 8.26 km² site beside a KTX station, fully usable only once the airbase and civil airport leave[S3, S4, S5].

Summary

On 6 July 2026 the government chose the Gwangju military airport (광주 군공항) in Gwangsan-gu (광산구): about 2.5 million pyeong, or 8.26 km² (1 pyeong = 400/121 m²). The land is state-owned, flat and next to Gwangju Songjeong (광주송정) KTX station[S3]. On 29 July the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport (MOLIT) designated it a candidate national industrial complex and said it may expand it[S5]. The 8.26 km² comprises the airfield (1.85 M pyeong; 6.12 km²), a reserved ammunition-depot relocation site (0.24 M pyeong) and a safety zone (0.39 M pyeong). The OSM outline of the airbase measures 6.01 km², consistent with the airfield component[S4, S40]. Gwangju plans to develop about 630 000 pyeong (2.08 km²) first. No land price or appraisal has been published; under current law, the old site funds the new base through a donation-for-concession (기부 대 양여) swap that is now being amended[S7, S21]. Other candidates were smaller (Cheomdan 3-jigu (첨단3지구), 3.62 km²), largely greenbelt (Bitgreen (빛그린), Future Car), on reclaimed land (Solaseado (솔라시도)) or companion complexes (Naju Energy, Muan)[S10, S15, S16, S36, S38].

Assessment

▲ Advantage
Large, flat, state-owned site with no private compensation, next to KTX[S3, S4].
▼ Constraint
Military and civil aviation must leave first; zoning restrictions[S4, S10].
? Uncertainty
Expansion boundary; land-value basis for relocation funding[S5, S21].

Disputes and open questions

  • Expansion beyond four fabs is under review[S5]; scale and boundary of any extension are not published.
  • Full use requires both the military base and the civilian airport to leave; greenbelt, flight-safety and military protection zones must be lifted[S4].
  • No single official list naming all seven candidates was located; five were named in press comparisons[S10, S15] and two (Naju, Muan) are separate national-complex projects[S36, S38].
  • Reported areas differ slightly (8.2 km², 8.26 km², 820 vs 826 ×10⁴ m²)[S4, S5, S24].

F4 · Military airport relocation

Constraint

Temporary dispersal by mid-2028 bridges a permanent move to Muan that still needs a resident referendum[S17, S18, S20].

Summary

The selected land is still an active airbase (1st Fighter Wing) shared with domestic civil flights[S4]. Two tracks run in parallel. First, a temporary dispersal: by mid-2028 the Ministry of National Defense (MND) plans to move the wing's training squadrons to Yecheon (예천) and its 206th squadron to Seosan (서산), under review, making room by moving some Yecheon assets to Jungwon (중원, Chungju). Construction is expected from 2027[S17]. Second, permanent relocation: Mangun-myeon (망운면), Muan (무안), was named preliminary candidate on 2 April 2026 and relocation candidate on 28 August 2026, with the county head's conditional consent. A resident referendum (about 77 000 voters) was expected around October 2026, and the government aims to fix the final site in 2026 and finish the new base by December 2032[S19, S18, S20]. As of 13 October 2026 no referendum result had been reported in the sources reviewed. Civil domestic flights are to move to Muan International Airport, which has been closed since the December 2024 Jeju Air crash and needs runway repairs until the second half of 2027[S25].

Assessment

▲ Advantage
Relocation process already at candidate stage, with temporary dispersal as a bridge[S17, S18].
▼ Constraint
Fab construction depends on vacating an active airbase; law change pending[S4, S21].
? Uncertainty
Muan referendum outcome and timing; pilot-training disruption[S17, S20].

Disputes and open questions

  • Duplicate investment: critics note that temporary bases would be used for about four years before a second move to Muan[S17].
  • Muan county set three preconditions (civil airport first, ₩1 trillion support guaranteed, national incentives) and boycotted two committee meetings; a residents' committee demanded the support be written down before the vote[S18, S20].
  • Current law (donation-for-concession) does not allow the old site to be developed before the move; an amendment for early transfer and state funding was under discussion in the regular session[S21].
  • Rep. Kim Do-eup (PPP) said the feasibility-waiver application presented the relocation as decided when it was not[S33].

F5 · Terrain, geology and hazards

Mixed

Low recorded seismicity, but river-flood risk areas adjoin the site and no site-specific survey is planned[S22, S23].

Summary

An analysis of the Korea Meteorological Administration catalogue since 1978, centred on the Gwangju airport reference point, counts 22 earthquakes of M ≥ 2.0 within 50 km, the largest M 3.2 (2013), and none of M ≥ 4.0. Within 100 km the largest was the M 4.8 Buan (부안) earthquake of 2024[S22]. A Chonnam National University geologist said no active faults are known in Gwangju–Jeonnam, but the integrated city has no plan for site-specific seismic verification. Critics note that fabs need site investigations of bedrock, liquefaction, ground amplification and micro-vibration[S22]. The site is flat, but the aircraft operations still on it are incompatible with vibration-sensitive lithography, which is why early relocation is a precondition[S10]. Flooding is the clearer hazard: on 7–8 August 2020, 516 mm of rain overtopped the Yeongsan basin in Gwangju (2 dead, 1 missing, 915 houses flooded). The Gwangju Research Institute lists Usan-dong (우산동), Songjeong Station, Suwan-dong (수완동) and Bollyang-dong (본량동), all near the site, among river-flood risk areas[S23]. Site-specific groundwater and typhoon-exposure data were not publicly reported.

Assessment

▲ Advantage
Low recorded seismicity; no known active faults; flat, already-graded land[S22, S3].
▼ Constraint
Adjacent river-flood risk zones; airbase vibration until vacated[S23, S10].

F6 · Transport and logistics

Mixed

Strong rail access; far from the capital-region base, with the regional airport closed until repairs finish in 2027[S24, S25].

Summary

The site is about 2 km from Gwangju Songjeong Station, the hub of the Honam (호남) high-speed line, with KTX-Cheongryong services to Seoul Yongsan in about 1 h 30 min and KTX-Sancheon under 2 h[S24]. The Honam Expressway and the 2nd Ring Road run alongside it[S16]. Straight-line distances computed from mapped coordinates are 230 km to the Yongin (용인) cluster, 184 km to Samsung's Onyang (온양) campus in the Chungcheong (충청) packaging hub, and 262 km to Incheon Airport[S40, S41]. Air links are in flux: Muan International Airport (41 km) has been closed since December 2024 and is due to reopen after runway repairs in the second half of 2027. Gwangju's domestic flights are to move there by end-2027, and the Honam KTX phase 2 through Muan Airport to Mokpo is targeted for December 2027[S25, S4]. Freight plans specific to semiconductors (air cargo, ports) were not publicly reported.

F7 · Human capital

Mixed

Universities are building pipelines while young adults continue to leave Gwangju and Jeonnam[S26, S27].

Summary

Talent supply is contested. Some argue Honam lies beyond a 'southern limit' for semiconductor engineers. Jeonnam gained 2 000 overall but still lost people in their 20s (−3.1 %)[S27].

F8 · Industrial ecosystem

Mixed

Back-end packaging and AI computing are present; front-end suppliers are expected, not committed[S1, S28, S29].

Summary

Gwangju already hosts back-end capacity. Amkor Technology Korea, the city's largest exporter, announced on 30 June 2026 more than ₩1 trillion for advanced-packaging lines in Gwangju, starting with ₩0.5 trillion in October 2026, and expects over 1 000 jobs[S28]. The National AI Data Center (88.5 PF; operating since October 2023) sits in Cheomdan 3-jigu, about 13 km north of the site[S29, S40]. Nearby complexes (Cheomdan, Jingok (진곡), Hanam (하남), Pyeongdong (평동), Bitgreen) cover R&D, materials and equipment, and general manufacturing[S16]. Front-end (fab) suppliers are not yet present; the expectation that materials, parts and equipment firms will follow the anchors is a projection, not a commitment[S1]. Nationally, the 29 June plan assigns roles: Honam (southwest) as the second memory-production base (Samsung 2 fabs, SK hynix 2 fabs), Chungcheong as the packaging hub (₩81 trillion, including HBM fabs at Onyang/Cheonan and packaging at Cheongju (청주)), and the southeast and Daegu–Gyeongbuk regions as materials, parts and equipment hubs[S1, S2]. Muan's new national-complex candidate is intended for semiconductor materials, parts and equipment linked to the Gwangju complex[S38].

▲ Advantage
Existing OSAT anchor (Amkor) and national AI data center near the site[S28, S29].

Pace of supplier relocation; fab count beyond four[S1, S5].

F9 · Policy and political geography

Mixed

Strong legal and fiscal backing, contested along regional and partisan lines[S30, S33, S34].

Summary

The project is framed as balanced national development, ending the capital-region 'single pole'[S1]. Three legal changes underpin it. The Semiconductor Special Act (enacted 10 February 2026; enforcement decree in force 11 August 2026) prioritises non-capital regions for cluster designation and lets the state and local governments fund 50–100 % of infrastructure, and all of it for redundancy facilities[S30]. Gwangju and Jeollanam-do, separated in 1986, merged into the Jeonnam–Gwangju Integrated Special City (전남광주통합특별시) on 1 July 2026 (about 3.2 million people)[S31]. The power-grid project received a preliminary-feasibility waiver on 1 September 2026[S12]. Contestation is regional and partisan. Jeonbuk (전북) groups seek distributed siting of follow-on fabs at Saemangeum (새만금). Yongin-area lawmakers warn against diverting capital-region investment[S6, S32, S35]. The PPP calls the selection political and demands the evaluation criteria; the government says it proposed sites and the companies chose[S34, S33]. The opposition also disputes whether firms or government picked the site[S11].

Assessment

▲ Advantage
Special Act funding rules favour non-capital regions; integrated city aligns local government[S30, S31].
▼ Constraint
Fast-tracked procedures (waivers, compressed environmental review) invite legal and political challenge[S3, S33].
? Uncertainty
Durability across future administrations; claims for distributed follow-on fabs[S34, S6].

Disputes and open questions

  • Political site selection: PPP floor leader Jeong Jeom-sik called the plan pre-campaigning in a DP stronghold and floated a national investigation; PK lawmakers demanded the selection criteria[S34].
  • Government position: Minister Kim Jung-kwan said the government proposed sites and the companies made the final decision; DP's Jang Cheol-min said expansion is not optional given demand[S33].
  • Who chose: Rep. Yoon Jae-ok (PPP) cited SK chair Chey Tae-won's July remark that the government judged Honam best[S11].
  • Saemangeum: Jeonbuk groups argue that, since Gwangju also needs new 345 kV lines, infrastructure gaps should not rule out Saemangeum for follow-on fabs[S6, S32].
  • Yongin: Rep. Yu Eui-dong (PPP) and Lee Jun-seok (Reform Party) demanded that Yongin's schedule not be diverted and that an objective site-evaluation table be published[S35].

Documentation

Methods, limitations and sources

Methodology and data sources

Research. The starting leads were treated as unverified. Each was checked against Korean- and English-language reporting retrieved on 13 October 2026, and every quantity was read in the article text rather than taken from search snippets. Government releases (Korea.kr) were used where located. Otherwise the report relies on national dailies, wire services (Yonhap, News1, Newsis) and regional outlets. Each factor draws on at least two independent outlets (see research/sources.md).

Attribution. Official decisions (designations, cabinet actions, enacted law) are distinguished from announced corporate plans and from targets. Contested claims are attributed to the person and party that made them. Quotations are avoided; Korean text is paraphrased.

Spatial data. Administrative boundaries are KOSTAT SGIS si-gun-gu polygons (2018 vintage) from the southkorea-maps repository, simplified with mapshaper[S39]. Feature coordinates come from OpenStreetMap elements[S40] or Wikipedia article coordinates[S41, S14]. Where a facility's exact location is unpublished, the marker uses the named administrative area's centroid or a schematic line and is flagged approximate (dashed). The airbase outline (OSM way 444717696) measures 6.01 km², which matches the 1.85 M pyeong airfield component reported in S4.

Derived values. Distances are great-circle (haversine, R = 6371.0088 km) from the vertex mean of the site outline (126.8024° E, 35.1217° N). Lines and polygons are measured to their nearest vertex. Area conversions use 1 pyeong = 400/121 m².

Charts. Every chart value is read at build time from data/factors.json, the same file that drives the tables and map, so each number has one source. Charts never combine values from different years into one bar. Targets are drawn hatched or dashed, projections in a light fill, and existing capacity solid.

Encoding. Each factor has one colour, used on the map, the factor grid, the timeline lanes and the synthesis matrix. Water is always ocean blue and power always electric blue; coral and amber mark dependencies and hazards. Assessments use shape as well as colour (▲ advantage, ▼ constraint, ◆ mixed).

Limitations and unverified items
  • Not publicly reported in any source reviewed: the land price or appraisal of the site; site groundwater conditions; site-specific typhoon exposure; freight routing for semiconductor inputs and outputs; a semiconductor-specific workforce requirement for the four fabs; the routes of the proposed 345 kV branch and of the water pipelines; the boundary of the 8.26 km² candidate complex; and the exact location of the Sin-Jangseong substation.
  • Not verified: no single official list naming all seven candidate sites was found. Five appear in press comparisons, and Naju Energy and Muan are separate national-complex projects[S10, S15, S36, S38]. The Muan referendum date and result were not reported as of 13 October 2026[S20]. Reports that the cluster could reach nine fabs, and totals of ₩896 trillion that include other investors, appeared in search results but were not confirmed in article text, so they are excluded.
  • Conflicting figures are shown side by side rather than reconciled: stage-1 line length (20–25 km, ≈32 km, ≈43 km in total) and capacity (3.1 GW vs ≈4 GW)[S7, S11, S12, S13]; the long-run water target (650 000 vs 1.06 M t/d)[S7, S8]; Solaseado's area (3.96 km² parcel vs 20.9 km² enterprise city)[S10, S15]; the Naju Energy complex budget (₩263.3 bn per MOLIT vs ₩368.5 bn per KBC)[S36, S37]; and the MOLIT designation date (29 vs 30 July)[S5, S6].
  • Source base. Most evidence is press reporting of official statements and parliamentary exchanges. Underlying documents (the NARS fact-check, KEPCO grid studies, MOLIT designation notice) were not obtained directly.
  • Geometry. The 2018 boundaries predate the 2026 merger and the reclaimed Saemangeum land. Islands under 0.5 km² were removed and lines simplified for display. Approximate markers show administrative areas, not facilities. Straight-line distances understate travel distances.
  • Basemap. CARTO tiles were planned, but on 13 October 2026 CARTO's keyless endpoint returned watermarked “API key required” tiles. The map uses the keyless Esri World Ocean Base instead (native detail to zoom 10, upscaled beyond). If the tiles fail, an embedded Korea land outline and all analytical layers still render.
